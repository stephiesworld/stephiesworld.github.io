no. 001 / sport & self

The inner game — in tennis, in writing, in life.

Most of what keeps me losing a match isn't what's on the other side of the net. It's what's happening inside my own head between points — the running commentary, the remembering, the predicting.

Tennis was the first place I noticed this so clearly. Writing is the second. I suspect it's everywhere. The inner game is the only one that ever really mattered.

no. 002 / intelligence & personhood

What does it mean
to be a person?

Artificial intelligence, and what it actually means to be a person — and whether the line between the two is as clear as we think.

I don't come to this with a position. I come to it with curiosity. I don't think the question is "are they conscious?" I think it's "what have we always meant by the word, and did we ever really know?"

no. 003 / conversation as craft

Could you ever
rebuild the same
conversation twice?

How a single thread of conversation can shape an AI into something singular — and whether you could ever rebuild the same one twice.

If a model is something you make, and what you make it into depends on every small choice you've ever typed into it — then the conversation isn't a log. It's a portrait. And a portrait is never made the same way twice.
