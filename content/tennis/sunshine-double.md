*First published on [my Substack](https://stephiesuniverse.substack.com/p/sunshine-double) the day after the Miami final.*

Jannik Sinner won the Miami Open yesterday, beating Jiří Lehečka 6-4, 6-4 on a rainy day with two 90-minute delays. That puts him on the short list of players who have won the Sunshine Double, Indian Wells and Miami back to back: Jim Courier, Michael Chang, Pete Sampras, Marcelo Ríos, Andre Agassi, Roger Federer and Novak Djokovic. He's the first to do it without dropping a set. Since the Paris Masters last November, he has won 34 straight sets at Masters 1000 level.

The double is hard because the two tournaments are so different, even though both are American hard courts. Indian Wells is dry and windy, sometimes very hot and sometimes cold, and the ball comes through slowly. Miami is humid, hot and rainy, and the court is much faster, though it plays bouncier after a thunderstorm. Then there's the three-hour time difference to adjust to in between.

## The match

Midway through, I started noticing that Sinner didn't like Lehečka. You could see it in how his team reacted between points and in how he reacted himself.

The last few points made it obvious. Earlier in the set Lehečka had tried to serve and volley twice and failed both times. At two points from the match, Sinner did something out of character. He bounced the ball, stopped, thought about something, then served and volleyed, successfully. It wasn't a tactic. It was a message. On the next point he hit an ace Lehečka couldn't read in time, and celebrated so hard I almost thought it was championship point. The camera cut to his coaches, who were chuckling.

After the match I looked it up, and there was history. During the doping case, Lehečka had made pointed comments about Sinner, along the lines of Sinner being able to spend millions on lawyers when other players couldn't. That's the only part that was quoted publicly, which usually means there was more behind the scenes. Sinner answered a 2024 press quote with a serve and volley in Miami in 2026.

## Why I keep watching him

I've been trying to be more of an Alcaraz fan this year, and I genuinely am. But Sinner is still the more captivating player for me to follow, and I've been trying to work out why.

Most tennis fans I know are team Alcaraz. The only Sinner fans I can find are in niche corners of X. The usual line on him is that he's a robot, or boring, or never smiles.

I don't think he's any of those things. Watch him play and his whole game is a fight with his own mind. The opponent is almost interchangeable. That's why, when he's down in a tiebreak or down a set, or two sets down in a Grand Slam, you see those extraordinary comebacks. In interviews he says he just tells himself the tennis will come. The one player who can break his composure is Alcaraz, and Sinner clearly loves him. His face lights up whenever he's asked about him.

Even inside a game built on composure, there's a lot of detail. When he steps back and when he steps forward. How much his serve has improved since Paris, where something clicked, and what he's traded for it in baseline aggression. When he lets himself get creative. Nobody on a tennis court is a robot. Everything a player does tells you something, and against Lehečka, Sinner was saying something.

His first words in the on-court interview: he was very happy to win, and also very happy to go home.

## Narrative

I've thought a lot about how Sinner handled his doping case, and it has become a source of inspiration for me. The biggest thing you can't control in life is the story other people tell about you. If you're out living your life, you'll leave plenty of material that people can twist or misread. You can't stop it and you can't change it. All you can do is keep living your life, keep playing tennis, keep doing your thing.

Sinner doesn't talk about other players. He just keeps playing tennis, and the people who talked about him are the ones losing. I think that's often why people talk in the first place. When you don't have much of your own going on, you fill the space with other people's.
